# Effects matrix

The canonical capability list is in `GOAL.md` section 10. The row-by-row implementation, automated-test, and device/listening status is tracked in [`IMPLEMENTATION_CHECKLIST.md`](IMPLEMENTATION_CHECKLIST.md). No effect is considered shipped merely because it appears in the catalog or UI.

For each implemented processor, record: stable ID/version; category and exact function; source revision and license; channel layout; parameters/defaults/units; sample rates; latency; tail and bypass policy; quality modes; resource cap; measured per-block CPU percentage; automated evidence; and manual listening/hardware evidence.

**Current picker update (2026-11-22):** The categorized picker has 73 entries. Megatap Delay is slot 68 under Delay, De-Esser is slot 69 under Vocal FX, and project-authored Dual-Engine Distortion is slot 70 under Drive & fuzz. `make web-test` passes the current native/browser target, including local-guitar Play smokes for all three effects. Megatap's 50 ms/four-tap smoke captured peak 0.8912509 / 47,400 active samples; De-Esser's 6.5 kHz smoke captured peak 0.4818548 / 47,999 active samples; Dual-Engine Distortion captured peak 0.8912509 / 47,999 active samples. The browser/native comparison excludes all three because the offline reference has none of these stages. Model-control and test limits are recorded in this matrix, `FLAGSHIP_EFFECT_COMPARISON.md`, `IMPLEMENTATION_CHECKLIST.md`, `AUDIO_COMPATIBILITY.md`, and `LATENCY_AND_QUALITY.md`.

## Implemented project-authored processors

These are project-authored processors except `poly-pitch-shifter`, which uses the pinned MIT STFT pitch-shift headers listed in [`THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.md`](THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.md). “Memory” is the processor object plus directly owned storage, excluding caller buffers, allocator metadata, and backend/runtime overhead. Linux CPU results are from the release processor-call benchmark and are not live-route measurements.

The current categorized browser picker has 73 entries, including project-authored stereo Auto-Pan in Modulation, a 1–64-tap Megatap Delay in Delay, a split-band De-Esser in Vocal FX, and Dual-Engine Distortion in Drive & fuzz, plus the mono/stereo Phrase Looper and Stereo Enhancer, Harmonic Tremolo, Synth-category tracked oscillator and free-running Note Generator, pitch-fed Shimmer Reverb and local-IR Convolution Reverb, Auto-Swell Delay, Pitch Feedback Delay, Resonant Delay, mono-tracked Feedback Generator, and five-band peaking EQ. The categorized Pitch stages include the Diatonic Harmonizer and Pitch Arpeggiator; Grain Delay remains in Delay. Tracked pitch/synth stages are experimental and do not claim manufacturer algorithm or tracking parity. Auto-Pan has project-authored ranges and DSP; Fender's analogous Orbit controls have no published numeric ranges/defaults/DSP details. The Dual-Engine Distortion exposes the control concepts listed for Fender Pugilist Distortion, but uses project-authored ranges and processing; guide v1.1.100 does not state numeric bounds or DSP, and current firmware availability is unverified. Megatap implements documented Fractal control concepts with project-authored ranges and processing, not vendor DSP/settings or device parity; exact FM9 Mark II Turbo firmware scope remains qualified. Prime's official product page lists De-Esser under Vocal FX but publishes no model controls/ranges; the project stage's six controls and DSP are independent. The arpeggiator supports ±7 scale degrees versus Fractal's documented ±24. Shimmer, Convolution Reverb, five-band EQ, Phrase Looper, and Auto-Swell Delay also remain project-authored implementations rather than named commercial algorithms/settings. The browser looper is one layer, capped at four seconds at 48 kHz, with no quantization or persistence; scheduling impact of its snapshots has not been measured. Synthetic full-chain benchmark and browser capture limitations are recorded in `LATENCY_AND_QUALITY.md` and `AUDIO_COMPATIBILITY.md`.

## Channel-layout utility (UTL-04)

`convert_channels` handles interleaved mono/stereo float audio outside the `Effect` interface because conversion can change buffer length. It copies same-layout data, duplicates mono into left/right, and supports average or equal-power stereo summing. The default average is conservative for correlated content; equal-power may exceed full scale and adds no limiter. Only 1↔2 channels are supported. Invalid layouts, incomplete input frames, undersized output spans, and partial overlaps fail without modifying output; exact-start in-place conversion is supported. The operation is stateless, has no owned memory or algorithmic latency, and performs no allocation. Linux 48 kHz stereo/128-frame benchmark: stereo→mono average p50/p95 0.0011/0.0015%, stereo→mono equal-power 0.0011/0.0015%, mono→stereo 0.0030/0.0030% of the 2.667 ms block deadline. CTest, offline CLI, browser/WASM startup self-check, and Windows cross-build cover the implementation; live hardware and listening remain open.

## Parallel routing utility (UTL-05)

`split_to_parallel` copies mono/stereo interleaved float frames into two caller-owned branch buffers. `ParallelMixer` combines dry audio with two branch inputs using independent branch/dry gains, linear stereo balance and per-branch polarity inversion. Dry-only is the safe default. Gain, balance, and polarity changes ramp over 10 ms by default (configurable); mono ignores balance. The splitter and mixer are stateless with respect to audio history, add no algorithmic latency, perform no allocation, and reject invalid layouts, incomplete frames, undersized buffers, or partial overlap; exact-start input/output aliasing is supported where documented. Mixer state is 112 B; split uses 0 B. CTest covers mono/stereo copies and mix, dry default, gains, balance, phase inversion, variable-block ramps, bounds, alias rules, invalid input, and allocation count. The WASM startup self-check covers split and phase-mixed output. Linux 48 kHz stereo/128-frame p50/p95 is 0.0019/0.0019% for split and 0.0053/0.0071% for mix. These are low-level utilities only: they are not yet connected to the preset graph/editor or live routing scheduler. Windows cross-build passes; browser parity currently checks startup behavior rather than user-created parallel routes. Manual hardware/listening remains open.

## Chromatic tuner utility (UTL-09)

The tuner analyzes a mono sum of the unprocessed input through a decimated, fixed-storage YIN-style difference search. Its candidate pitch range is 50–1500 Hz, with an adjustable A4 reference from 400–480 Hz (default 440 Hz); this is not polyphonic note detection or pitch shifting. Synthetic native tests pass at 55 Hz, 1500 Hz, and low E2; behavior at the 50 Hz boundary and on real guitar/polyphonic input remains unverified. At 48 kHz the analysis window is 1,024 samples at 12 kHz (85.3 ms), with bounded analysis work spread across audio callbacks. It adds no algorithmic delay to audible processing. Optional output mute is applied after the output limiter so it also silences delay tails, and transitions ramp over 10 ms. The Linux tuner state is 10,400 B; the WASM tuner object reports 10,364 B and is included in the browser chain total. On the Linux 48 kHz stereo/128-frame benchmark it uses 0.0083% p50, 0.4358% p95, and 0.9405% maximum of the 2.667 ms callback deadline; transient peaks reflect the bounded analysis batches. Native tests cover note/frequency/cents, low E2, reference limits, silence, stereo, mute ramps, and zero allocations. ## Shuffling Looper (Loop & performance)

The project-authored Shuffling Looper is a separate browser/WASM stage informed by the Helix Stadium XL manual selector, which identifies firmware v1.4.1 / Rev. E. The linked Looper Block subpage still displays a Rev D header; the current v1.4 content was checked 2026-11-22. The manual names Slices, SeqLength, Shuffle, Pitch, Reverse, Repeat, Smoothing, Seq Drift, Playback, Low Cut, High Cut, and Interval 1/2. Shuffle, Pitch, Reverse, Repeat, and Seq Drift are described as likelihood controls; 0–100% is given for Shuffle, Reverse, Repeat, and Seq Drift, while Pitch's numeric range is unclear. Intervals cover one octave down/up. Numeric limits/defaults for most other values remain unpublished. Stadium's Shuffling Looper allows one block per preset, up to 60 s mono or 30 s stereo, clears on power-off, and is listed for Stadium hardware rather than Stadium Native. This stage uses explicit Record, Stop recording + play, Play, Stop, Randomize, and Clear buttons instead of the device's single-switch press/double-press/hold gestures. It replaces the previous recording, stores at most 192,000 frames (4 s at 48 kHz), and is not saved to a preset or file. The slice/sequence algorithm, bounds/defaults, and DSP are project-authored, with no Line 6 code or sonic/control parity claimed. Native tests cover sequence and transport state, bounds, reset, and finite output; browser tests verify picker categorization, WASM command/options serialization, and transport playback/status. The stage adds no fixed dry-path latency; its loop buffer is fixed at construction, and no isolated CPU benchmark or human/device listening check has been run.

The June 2023 Fractal Audio Blocks Guide (pp. 92–93) covers FM9 and documents Ten-Tap Delay and separate Rhythm Tap Delay.
