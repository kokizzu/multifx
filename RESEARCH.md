# Research and source audit

Research baseline: `GOAL.md` was supplied with a source bibliography dated 20 November 2026. Its product-reference and component tables are research leads, not evidence that this repository has integrated or revalidated those sources.

## Required audit before adoption

- Reconfirm exact upstream commit/tag, source files, full license text, transitive dependencies, supported architectures, sample-rate behavior, algorithmic delay, CPU/memory requirements, and parameter semantics.
- Preserve GPLv3-compatible distribution; exclude GPL-2-only, unknown-license code, proprietary binaries, and assets without redistribution rights unless a compatible grant is verified.
- Record upstream links and exact versions in `THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.md`; record integration changes and build recipe here.
- The Amp Rack audit in `GOAL.md` identifies a pinned source snapshot and warns that bundled binary libraries, commented-out source modules, and individual effect licenses must be considered separately.
- Pitch candidates in `GOAL.md` are not yet integrated, built, or measured in this project. Do not treat library buffer/window values as end-to-end latency.

## BBD-style delay research (DLY-06, 2026-11-21)

BOSS's official DM-2W product page identifies its analog delay line as BBD-based and describes Standard mode as the shorter, warmer 20–300 ms sound and Custom mode as a clearer analog sound with up to 800 ms. This supports keeping warm-repeat coloration distinct from clean digital delay; it does not specify a digital emulation algorithm. TabletFX therefore uses a clearly labeled project-authored approximation: a 4.5 kHz one-pole low-pass (capped at 0.45× sample rate) and mild rational saturation on the delay-ring write signal, with a 10 ms style blend. The immediate dry signal is unchanged; no analog noise, clock artifacts, compander behavior, or circuit-level BBD stages are claimed. Native impulse/stereo/no-allocation tests, CLI coverage, browser WASM parity, Linux benchmarks, and Windows cross-build provide implementation evidence; guitar listening remains open.

- **DLY-06 product-character reference:** [BOSS DM-2W product page](https://www.boss.info/global/products/dm-2w/).

## Tape Echo research (DLY-07, 2026-11-21)

The official BOSS RE-202 page exposes Saturation and Wow & Flutter as separate controls and describes their roles as magnetic-tape/preamp compression-colour and tape modulation. The official Strymon El Capistan manual also describes Wow & Flutter as speed fluctuation, while Tape Age changes bandwidth and Tape Crinkle models irregularities. These references support keeping saturation and time modulation as independently adjustable controls; they do not specify a generic tape emulation algorithm or authorize a claim of machine accuracy. TabletFX adds a project-authored approximation to its existing Delay ring: a rational saturation curve on the ring-write/feedback signal plus two linked sine LFOs that modulate fractional delay reads with linear interpolation. Defaults are 45% saturation, 0.35 ms wow at 0.30 Hz, and 0.08 ms flutter at 6 Hz; style/control changes ramp over 10 ms. The DLY-07 coloration itself has no tape hiss, head geometry, bias, speed-dependent frequency response, or machine-specific wow/flutter spectra; DLY-08 adds a separate generic playback-head pattern. Automated impulse, modulation, stereo-link, transition-smoothing, finite-output and allocation tests pass; browser/native parity, five prerecorded clean pickup-position renders, stereo DI music rendering, Linux CPU/memory benchmarks, and Windows cross-build pass. Human tape/guitar listening remains open.

- **DLY-07 references:** [BOSS RE-202](https://www.boss.info/global/products/re-202/), [Strymon El Capistan user manual](https://www.strymon.net/manuals/ElCapistan_v2_UserManual_RevB.pdf), and [El Capistan product page](https://www.strymon.net/product/elcapistan/).

## Multi-head tape echo research (DLY-08, 2026-11-21)

Roland's official RE-202 Reference Manual documents playback heads 2, 3, and 4 at 2×, 3×, and 4× the head-1 delay time; its modes 8–12 use head 4, which was absent from the RE-201. Roland's official RE-20 Owner's Manual documents a different three-head RE-201 pattern family, including single-head and combined-head modes. These sources establish that multi-head combinations and ratios vary by product; they do not establish one universal head pattern or level/pan law.

TabletFX's DLY-08 is a project-authored four-head spacing pattern. A base spacing selects head delays at 1×, 2×, 3×, and 4×; the user chooses any subset. Selected heads are normalized to equal level and alternate between fixed stereo positions. Shared delay feedback and wet mix remain the master controls. The pattern reuses the preallocated four-tap delay ring and existing transition ramps, adds no processor stage or process-time allocation, and keeps DLY-07 saturation/wow/flutter independently selectable. It does not reproduce the RE-202 or RE-201 mode table, head gains, reverb combinations, or tape-machine behavior.

- **DLY-08 references:** [Roland RE-202 Reference Manual, “Head Combinations for Each Mode”](https://static.roland.com/manuals/re-202_reference/eng/25633275.html) and [BOSS RE-20 Owner's Manual](https://cdn.roland.com/assets/media/pdf/RE-20_OM.pdf) (printed pp. 18–19, “About the Variation Mode” and “About the Playback Heads”).
