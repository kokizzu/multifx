# Research and source audit

Research baseline: `GOAL.md` was supplied with a source bibliography dated 20 November 2026. Its product-reference and component tables are research leads, not evidence that this repository has integrated or revalidated those sources.

## Required audit before adoption

- Reconfirm exact upstream commit/tag, source files, full license text, transitive dependencies, supported architectures, sample-rate behavior, algorithmic delay, CPU/memory requirements, and parameter semantics.
- Preserve GPLv3-compatible distribution; exclude GPL-2-only, unknown-license code, proprietary binaries, and assets without redistribution rights unless a compatible grant is verified.
- Record upstream links and exact versions in `THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.md`; record integration changes and build recipe here.
- The Amp Rack audit in `GOAL.md` identifies a pinned source snapshot and warns that bundled binary libraries, commented-out source modules, and individual effect licenses must be considered separately.
- Pitch candidates in `GOAL.md` are not yet integrated, built, or measured in this project. Do not treat library buffer/window values as end-to-end latency.

## Fender Tone Master Pro Stereo Tape Echo control check (2026-11-23)

The official Tone Master Pro Model Guide v1.1.100, pp. 49–50, lists Stereo Tape Echo controls for independent left and right delay times and tap divisions. Feedback, Mix, Record Level, Tape Bias, Tape Noise, Wow and Flutter, and Spillover are shared controls. The guide warns that high feedback may self-oscillate and gives no numeric bounds, defaults, or DSP topology. Fender's current firmware page lists v1.8.85, dated 2026-11-18, but its release notes do not mention Stereo Tape Echo; exact applicability of the older model-guide revision to that firmware remains unconfirmed. TabletFX now adds optional independent L/R time or tempo-division controls while retaining shared timing by default for existing presets. Its modes, ranges, defaults, smoothing, and signal processing are project-authored and do not claim Fender parity. Sources: [Tone Master Pro Model Guide v1.1.100](https://www.fmicassets.com/Damroot/Original/10018/OM_2274900000_TONE-MASTER-PRO-Model-Guide_English.pdf), [current Fender firmware notes](https://support.fender.com/hc/en-gb/articles/46768129660315-Fender-Tone-Master-Pro-Firmware-and-Pro-Control-App-Updates).

## Flagship effect-list audit: Axom Max, HeadRush Prime, and XR1 (2026-11-23)

This supplements the focused flagship comparison in `FLAGSHIP_EFFECT_COMPARISON.md`; it is not a complete audit of any device's library or of this repository's DSP.

These labels mix processing categories with amp/cab, loader, and routing blocks; the 19-vs-20 scope discrepancy is unresolved. The accessible material does not expose model-by-model effect names or controls/ranges. Sources: [Melofty downloads](https://www.meloftyaudio.com/support/downloads), [V1.7.4 user manual](https://cdn.rockboard.de/nos/data/erp/Artikell_PDF/Manual_PDF/Melofty/EN/MEL_AXOM_MAX_Manual_EN.pdf), and [secondary quick-guide transcription](https://manuals.plus/melofty/sft40-axom-max-hybrid-audio-processor).
- **HeadRush Prime:** The official downloads page lists firmware/user guide v5.2.0, with October 2026 release notes showing NAM enhancements and bug fixes, not new effects. The official product page exposes categorized model names (recorded in the comparison), but it has internal count conflicts: Overdrive displays 16 while listing 18, and Pitch displays 7 while listing duplicate `Octaves`; lower model cards identify eight distinct pitch entries. The 11.7 MB v5.2.0 manual could not be inspected with the available PDF reader. The readable v4.1 guide provides generic block operation rather than a complete model-specific parameter catalogue. Some Prime vocal-support articles and older editor examples remain specific verified exceptions; do not extend them to all effect models. - **XRecer XR1:** The manufacturer FAQ says the manual's Effects List has 111 effect-model entries, plus 4 IR Loader and 9 FX Loop entries. It names broad categories but the linked manual resolves to a wiki root without readable effect tables. As of 2026-11-23 the first-batch shipping estimate is 2026-12-09, so the XR1 had not yet shipped. The founder letter describes firmware 1.1 vocal processing at shipment and later effects/synth work as future plans. No factory algorithm name or per-effect parameter list is verified. Sources: [XR1 FAQ](https://www.xrecer.com/pages/faq), [XR1 product page](https://www.xrecer.com/products/xr1), [download center](https://www.xrecer.com/pages/download), and [founder's letter](https://www.xrecer.com/blogs/article/founder-letter).

Implementation consequence: the existing categorized picker covers broad families, but family membership is not model parity. Do not create Axom/Prime/XR1-labeled DSP or copy numerical settings until a readable exact-model manual or device evidence supports them. Implement independent project-authored features only where controls and behavior can be tested; leave exact vendor controls, ranges, and tracking/synth behavior open otherwise.

## Project-authored Stereo Enhancer

The June 2023 [Fractal Audio Systems Blocks Guide](https://www.fractalaudio.com/downloads/manuals/fas-guides/Fractal-Audio-Blocks-Guide.pdf) lists three Enhancer types and two FM9 instances. It describes Modern frequency-based L/R separation with Width, Depth, Low Cut, and High Cut; Classic right-channel delay (Width) from 0–20 ms with Phase Invert, Pan Left/Right, and Balance; and Stereoizer using multiple high-order filters, with Depth for band count and Width for spread. The checked section gives no numeric ranges for those controls beyond Classic’s delay range. Fractal says the block has Level and Bypass on Config and no Mix page, and that Modern can convert mono to stereo. The November guide is family documentation, not Mark II Turbo-specific firmware evidence; FM9 v12.0 downloads list Original/Turbo without naming Mark II Turbo separately, and installed firmware is unknown.

TabletFX adds a project-authored Stereo Enhancer in the Stereo & image picker category. Its Modern mode uses an M/S side-band processor; Classic delays the right channel; Stereoizer creates an all-pass-derived side signal. Project controls are Width 0–2, Depth 0–1, Low/High Cut, Classic delay 0–20 ms, phase inversion, left/right pan, balance, and Mix. Except for the documented Classic delay limit, bounds/defaults are project choices. Mono buffers remain mono and are sanitized, so the implementation does not provide Fractal’s mono-to-stereo conversion. The project’s Mix slider is not a Fractal control, and the independent DSP does not claim model parity. make test passed 68 CTest cases and offline CLI checks; make web-test passed the categorized browser control path and native/WASM Play comparison with Enhancer bypassed. Focused device listening and isolated CPU measurement remain open.

## Flagship pitch arpeggiator comparison

The June 2023 Fractal Audio Blocks Guide documents its FM9 Pitch-block Arpeggiator as one voice with 16 scale-aware steps, shifts up to ±24 scale degrees, and no voice delay. Named controls are Key, Scale, Run, Trigger Thresh, Steps, Repeats, Tempo, amplitude/pan Shape and Alpha, and per-step Shift. The guide does not give numeric limits for most controls. Fractal's FM9 downloads page lists v12.0 for “FM9 Original and FM9 Turbo”; that phrasing does not separately establish FM9 Mark II Turbo compatibility or a unit's installed firmware.

TabletFX now has a project-authored 16-step Pitch Arpeggiator in the Pitch category. Its project controls include ±7 scale degrees, 1–16 steps, repeats, manual/threshold triggering, free/tempo divisions, amplitude/pan shape and alpha, Mix, and Level. Threshold bounds (−90…0 dBFS), 5 ms detector attack, 80 ms release, and other bounds are project choices, not Fractal values. The implementation shares the monophonic Diatonic Harmonizer path and therefore has about 26.7 ms estimated first-note response at 48 kHz and 1,024,888 B measured stereo state. The 48 kHz stereo/128-frame Linux benchmark recorded 15.4107% p50 and 17.4398% p95 of a 2.667 ms deadline, with a 52.1921% maximum. Treat that cost as a live-path optimization issue. `make test`, `make web-test`, `make audio-regression`, and `make bench` pass; the 55-stage browser test confirms Play/native numerical parity, not sound quality. No Fractal DSP, note-tracking, threshold-range, or performance parity is claimed.

## Project-authored free-running Note Generator

The categorized Synth picker now includes a project-authored free-running oscillator source distinct from the guitar-tracked Mono Guitar Synth. Line 6’s [Helix Stadium drone tutorial](https://blog.line6.com/2026/08/17/perfect-practice-with-helix-stadium-part-2-creating-drones-and-harmony-beds/) uses the 3 Note Generator with its input set to None and documents Osc Shape, Note, Octave, and Level controls. The article gives no numeric bounds. TabletFX extends those concepts with one to three fixed oscillators, project-selected waveform/note/octave/level, and summed output level; it does not track guitar notes, accept MIDI note input, reproduce Line 6 DSP, or claim manufacturer parity. ## Project-authored Mono Guitar Synth

The six-model flagship comparison documents several distinct synth designs (manufacturer categories, oscillator synths, polysynths, Hybrid/DeepSynth systems, and a not-yet-released XR1 synth) but does not establish identical pitch tracking or shared parameter ranges. The first incremental TabletFX implementation is therefore a deliberately generic, project-authored monophonic guitar-tracked oscillator, not any of those products' synth algorithm. It is in a dedicated Synth picker category and supports Sine, Triangle, Saw, and fixed Square waveform choices; Mix, Oscillator Level, Gate Threshold, Attack, Release, and Portamento. The fixed Square has no pulse-width control. The tracker covers approximately 55–1,100 Hz and uses a 1,024-sample analysis frame (~21.3 ms at 48 kHz); that frame size does not prove real-guitar accuracy or full response latency. It does not support played chords as separate notes or instrument samples.

Native CTest/offline CLI checks validate explicit opt-in, a synthetic 220 Hz output, invalid waveform rejection, stereo behavior, control bounds, and reset. The 56-stage HeadlessChrome 151 browser test passed category/control checks and Play/native numerical parity (maximum absolute error 0.0000250265; RMS error 0.00000515164; local-file decoding and processed playback capture passed). Ryzen 9 5950X/GCC 13.3 Release benchmark at 48 kHz stereo/128-frame blocks recorded 11.8248% p50 / 15.4065% p95 / 26.4458% maximum of a 2.667 ms callback deadline, with 1,024,760 B owned state. These are synthetic-path checks and processor-call measurements. Prerecorded regression now passes with the synth present across five pickup positions and the stereo DI, but full-chain output RMS is only about 1.46e-5 on bridge and 1.16e-5 on stereo DI. This does not establish a useful musical signal, real-guitar synth tracking, or sound quality. Device/listening tests and any flagship note-tracking, sound, or live-load parity remain open.

Primary sources: [Fractal Audio Blocks Guide](https://www.fractalaudio.com/downloads/manuals/fas-guides/Fractal-Audio-Blocks-Guide.pdf) and [FM9 downloads/firmware](https://www.fractalaudio.com/fm9-downloads/), checked 2026-11-22.

## Project-authored Pitch Feedback Delay

The first incremental implementation in the Delay category is a project-authored feedback topology: each delayed pass goes through the pinned `PolyPitchShifter` primitive before returning to the feedback ring, so repeats accumulate the selected interval. Controls are Time 40–6,000 ms (350 ms default), Feedback 0–0.95 (0.35), Mix 0–1 (0.25), and Pitch per repeat −12…+12 semitones (+7). These choices are not copied from any flagship product, and the implementation is not a model, control, or sound match for a named commercial effect. The scheduler compensates the shifter's reported latency when targeting repeat-peak spacing, but the wet onset/transient remains shifter-derived; the dry path is immediate. Browser disable resets the internal delay rather than preserving spillover.

Native tests cover repeat timing/pitch, feedback/mix, bounds, block partitioning, reset, finite recovery, and allocation behavior. make web-test passed the 58-stage HeadlessChrome 151 control/category and Play/native capture check; maximum absolute error was 0.00002568498 and RMS error was 0.00000416202. make audio-regression passed five EGFxSet pickup-position renders and stereo/mono Guitar-TECHS DI renders with processors=58; bridge/stereo-DI/mono-DI output RMS remains extremely low and establishes bounded processing only. On Ryzen 9 5950X/GCC 13.3 Release at 48 kHz stereo/128 frames, the 6 s-capacity processor owns 2,798,168 B and measures 11.7123% p50 / 14.6276% p95 / 26.7206% maximum of a 2.667 ms callback deadline. The synthetic 58-stage stress chain maximum reached 101.9552%, slightly exceeding one callback; this is not a live device measurement. Listening, hardware, and useful output-level verification remain open.

## Line 6 Helix Stadium Feedbacker source check

The current [Helix Stadium model catalogue](https://line6.com/helix-stadium-models/) lists `Feedbacker` as mono, a Line 6 Original feedback generator, with initial appearance 1.0. The current [Stadium effect-block manual](https://manuals.line6.com/en/helix-stadium/live/effect-blocks) gives only shared parameter guidance, not a Feedbacker-specific parameter table. A separate official [Line 6 Helix software effect description](https://line6.com/software/index.html?locale=nl_BE&name=Helix+Native&os=All&submit_form=set) lists Fdbk Gain and Fdbk Type (−Octave, Unison, +Octave, Oct +5th, +2 Octaves, 2 Oct+3rd, 2 Oct+5th, 2 Oct+7th, Mid to Low, High to Low, Rndm Onset, Rndm Trigger); Attack; Release; Dry Kill (Off/On/Always); Dry Level; Reference (Lowest/Loudest); Silence Thr; Onset Thr; Offset Thr; Retrigger; and Trails. It says the model loads bypassed, works best early in the chain, warns gain can overwhelm the instrument, and recommends momentary Retrigger assignment. The shared Stadium manual says Feedbacker is excluded from the Dynamics sidechain option and its Rev D v1.3 DSP note includes Feedbacker in the group limited to one per path. Numeric ranges are unpublished. The software page's detailed control list is family-level evidence and is not proof that every control or setting is identical on current Stadium XL firmware.

The project now includes feedback-generator as a separately labeled, project-authored Dynamics stage. It tracks one note using the existing monophonic guitar-synth detector, generates a sine sustain, and offers Unison, Octave down, or Octave up. Project controls are Generated Level 0–1 (default 0.35), Attack 0–2,000 ms (15), Release 0–5,000 ms (600), Dry Kill Off/On (default Off), Dry Level 0–1 (default 1), and Silence Threshold −90…0 dBFS (−48). These ranges and defaults are not Line 6 values. The stage hard-limits output to ±1.0 and is bypassed by default. It omits chord-reference selection, the other Feedbacker harmonic modes, randomized onset/trigger, onset/offset thresholds, Dry Kill Always, Retrigger, and Trails; its fixed chain placement is late despite Line 6's early-placement guidance. The tracker's approximately 55–1,100 Hz candidate range and 1,024-sample frame do not establish input polyphony, accurate guitar tracking, or full response latency. The project does not claim Line 6 DSP, settings, tracking, or sound parity.

## Project-authored Shimmer Reverb

The six-model Shimmer/convolution check is recorded in [`FLAGSHIP_EFFECT_COMPARISON.md`](FLAGSHIP_EFFECT_COMPARISON.md). Fender's Tone Master Pro Model Guide v1.1.100 names Shimmer Reverb and its Mix, Decay, Damping, Diffuse, Feedback, High Pass Filter, and Spillover controls, without numeric ranges; current checked firmware is v1.8.85 (2026-11-18), and v1.6.48 records a Shimmer Damping fix. The Helix Stadium model catalogue lists mono/stereo Shimmer from firmware 1.0, while its effect-block manual gives only common reverb controls where supported. The June 2023 Fractal family Blocks Guide puts Shimmer Verb among Plex Delay types and names Shimmer Intensity 0–10 and Forward/Reverse Direction; its ±24-semitone Shift 1–8 controls are under the separate Plex Shift type and are not attributed to Shimmer Verb. Prime lists a HeadRush Original Shimmer but no Shimmer-specific control table in the reviewed v5.0.0 manual; its C-Verb convolution effect has separate file, High Pass, Low Pass, Wet, and Tails controls and a 10-second IR ceiling. XR1's FAQ does establish single/dual Reverb IR Loader entries with 24-bit/48 kHz WAV files of 2,048 samples; its product remains pre-order as of this check. These are source findings, not evidence of any unit's installed firmware or a complete catalogue audit.

TabletFX now implements a separate, project-authored `shimmer-reverb` stage in the Reverb category. It uses the existing plate reverb and pitch shifter, filters and returns the pitched wet signal through a bounded 128-frame ring, then blends the plate and pitch-shifted wet output. Project bounds/defaults are recorded in `EFFECTS_MATRIX.md`; they are not copied manufacturer settings. The direct dry path is immediate, with 2.667 ms at 48 kHz added to the feedback return before the shifter's wet latency. Browser bypass clears the tail, so Shimmer spillover is not implemented. Native, offline CLI, categorized 60-stage WASM/browser capture and `make audio-regression` checks pass; the current 60-stage browser/native capture error is 0.0000160764 maximum absolute and 0.0000025210 RMS. The guitar regression remains very low-level, so it establishes stable processing and numerical parity only. No commercial algorithm, settings, or sound parity is claimed; manual listening and hardware checks remain open.

### Project-authored Convolution Reverb

The Reverb picker has a local user-IR convolver, separate from the Shimmer effect. It uses uniform 128-frame overlap-add partitions, a 24,000-frame target-rate IR cap, load-time linear resampling, and an aligned 128-frame dry path when an IR is loaded. The browser accepts local mono/stereo WAV data; mono IR is duplicated and stereo IR is processed as independent left/right channels. Project-authored controls now include Mix, Wet Level, wet High Pass and wet Low Pass filters, plus a per-block Tails toggle. With Tails On, bypass passes the dry chain and adds the stored convolved wet tail; with Tails Off, pending convolution state is cleared. The filter ranges/defaults are project choices because the HeadRush guide does not publish numeric bounds. No IR is bundled and no commercial processor is emulated.

Manufacturer comparison: Fender documents convolution-reverb models and a one-per-preset limit; the examined HeadRush Prime v5.0.0 guide documents C-Verb's Preset, Reverb File, High Pass, Low Pass, Wet and Tails controls, one instance per rig, first-two-channel behavior for multichannel IRs, a 10-second maximum IR, and CPU use proportional to IR length. The official downloads page lists a v5.2 user guide, but its 11.7 MB PDF could not be text-inspected; v5.2.0 firmware notes (October 2026) discuss NAM additions/improvements and do not name C-Verb changes. XR1's FAQ specifies 24-bit/48 kHz WAV and 2,048 samples. Exact vendor algorithms and control equivalence remain unclaimed. TabletFX still caps IRs at 24,000 target-rate frames (0.5 s at 48 kHz), supports mono/stereo rather than using the first two channels of arbitrary multichannel files, and has no rig-switch spillover. See `AUDIO_COMPATIBILITY.md`, `EFFECTS_MATRIX.md`, and `LATENCY_AND_QUALITY.md` for the full measurements and remaining listening/device checks.

## BBD-style delay research (DLY-06, 2026-11-21)

BOSS's official DM-2W product page identifies its analog delay line as BBD-based and describes Standard mode as the shorter, warmer 20–300 ms sound and Custom mode as a clearer analog sound with up to 800 ms. The immediate dry signal is unchanged; no analog noise, clock artifacts, compander behavior, or circuit-level BBD stages are claimed. Native impulse/stereo/no-allocation tests, CLI coverage, browser WASM parity, Linux benchmarks, and Windows cross-build provide implementation evidence; guitar listening remains open.

## Tape Echo research (DLY-07, 2026-11-21)

The official BOSS RE-202 page exposes Saturation and Wow & Flutter as separate controls and describes their roles as magnetic-tape/preamp compression-colour and tape modulation. The official Strymon El Capistan manual also describes Wow & Flutter as speed fluctuation, while Tape Age changes bandwidth and Tape Crinkle models irregularities. These references support keeping saturation and time modulation as independently adjustable controls; they do not specify a generic tape emulation algorithm or authorize a claim of machine accuracy. TabletFX adds a project-authored approximation to its existing Delay ring: a rational saturation curve on the ring-write/feedback signal plus two linked sine LFOs that modulate fractional delay reads with linear interpolation. Defaults are 45% saturation, 0.35 ms wow at 0.30 Hz, and 0.08 ms flutter at 6 Hz; style/control changes ramp over 10 ms. The DLY-07 coloration itself has no tape hiss, head geometry, bias, speed-dependent frequency response, or machine-specific wow/flutter spectra; DLY-08 adds a separate generic playback-head pattern. Automated impulse, modulation, stereo-link, transition-smoothing, finite-output and allocation tests pass; browser/native parity, five prerecorded clean pickup-position renders, stereo DI music rendering, Linux CPU/memory benchmarks, and Windows cross-build pass. Human tape/guitar listening remains open.

## Multi-head tape echo research (DLY-08, 2026-11-21)

Roland's official RE-202 Reference Manual documents playback heads 2, 3, and 4 at 2×, 3×, and 4× the head-1 delay time; its modes 8–12 use head 4, which was absent from the RE-201. Roland's official RE-20 Owner's Manual documents a different three-head RE-201 pattern family, including single-head and combined-head modes. These sources establish that multi-head combinations and ratios vary by product; they do not establish one universal head pattern or level/pan law.

TabletFX's DLY-08 is a project-authored four-head spacing pattern. A base spacing selects head delays at 1×, 2×, 3×, and 4×; the user chooses any subset. Selected heads are normalized to equal level and alternate between fixed stereo positions. Shared delay feedback and wet mix remain the master controls.
