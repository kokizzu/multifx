# TabletFX

An offline guitar multi-effects application for Android, Linux, and Windows, with a browser/WASM guitar-audio test page. The C++20 DSP core is shared across native builds and the browser AudioWorklet; native audio backends are Oboe, JACK/PipeWire, and WASAPI.

The categorized Delay picker includes a project-authored Filtered Delay with free millisecond timing or tempo sync at 20–300 BPM, including 1/32 and 1/64 divisions. The timing mode does not change the existing repeat Low/High Cut processing or direct dry path; these controls are project-authored and do not claim Axom Max model/DSP parity.

The Reverb picker includes project-authored Cloud Reverb, Shimmer Reverb, Celestial-style dual-pitch feedback reverb, and user-IR Convolution Reverb. Cloud exposes Mix, Tone, Decay, Diffuse, High/Low Damp, Rate, Depth, and bypass Spillover controls; its ranges, defaults, and DSP are project-authored, with no Fender algorithm parity claim. The Celestial-style stage follows the control names in Fender Tone Master Pro Model Guide v1.1.100, but its numeric bounds/defaults, plate network, two pitch returns, and bypass spillover behavior are independently authored; it does not emulate Fender DSP. Fender's guide publishes no Celestial bounds or DSP details. The convolver accepts a local mono/stereo WAV up to 24,000 target-rate frames (0.5 s at 48 kHz), uses fixed 128-frame partitions with an aligned dry path, and exposes project-authored wet high-pass, wet low-pass, Wet Level, Mix, and per-block Tails controls. Tails On keeps the convolution tail audible when the block is bypassed; cross-rig spillover and Prime's documented 10-second IR limit are not implemented. No IR assets are bundled and no commercial algorithm is emulated.

The effect picker is grouped by type. Dynamics includes a project-authored three-band compressor with Low/Mid and Mid/High crossovers, 12/24 dB-per-octave slope, and separate Threshold, Ratio, Knee, Attack, Release, Level, Peak/RMS Detector, and Mute controls for each band. These are TabletFX controls; the implementation does not claim to clone a manufacturer's compressor. Drive & fuzz also includes a project-authored Bit Crusher with signed 2…24-bit quantization, per-channel sample hold (divisor 1…128), and wet mix. It intentionally aliases and has no selectable anti-alias filter; Fractal settings or DSP parity are not claimed.

Drive & fuzz also contains a project-authored Dual-Engine Distortion with separate Gain and Tone for engines A/B, Blend or Series routing, optional Bass Boost, and Level. The controls follow the concepts documented for Fender Tone Master Pro Pugilist Distortion, but its Model Guide v1.1.100 provides no numeric bounds/defaults or DSP details; TabletFX values and processing are independent, with no Fender parity claim.

Drive & fuzz also contains a project-authored Three-Stage Distortion informed by Line 6 Helix Stadium Mercator Dist control labels: Drive, Drive 2, Bass, Mid, Treble, Bright 1, Bright 2, and Level. Its three soft-clipping stages, tone shaping, bright boosts, ranges, and defaults are TabletFX choices; Line 6 publishes no numeric bounds or DSP topology for Mercator, and this is not a Line 6 model or sound match.

The separate Amp / preamp category contains a project-authored Porta Preamp inspired by the Line 6 Helix Stadium PortaBox 424 control labels: Gain 1, Gain 2, Bass, Treble, and Level. Line 6's v1.4 notes do not publish numerical ranges, defaults, or DSP details. TabletFX uses project ranges and defaults, two smoothed soft-clipping gain stages, and fixed bass/treble shelves; this is not an amp model, and it makes no Line 6 DSP or sound-parity claim.

Pitch includes a project-authored 16-step Pitch Sequence with per-step semitone shifts and Pitch, Glitch, or Freeze modes. Controls include 1–16 active steps, Run, free 20–2,000 ms or 30–300 BPM tempo timing, glide, glitch depth/interval, freeze-window length, Mix, and Level. Fender documents Pitch Sequencer as a Tone Master Pro effect in firmware v1.8.45 and describes pitch sequences, glides, glitches, and freeze-like behavior, but does not publish this model's controls, ranges, or DSP. TabletFX transposes incoming audio polyphonically without note detection or scale quantization; its Glitch mode adds deterministic random pitch offsets and Freeze repeats a short sampled loop, not a spectral freeze. Its values and processing are project-authored, with no Fender parity claim.

Modulation includes a project-authored Vibe processor using Rate, Depth, Trem, one/two Stages, stereo Spread, and Level, following the control names in Line 6 Helix Stadium's Essex Vibe release notes. The release notes do not publish numeric ranges, defaults, or DSP topology; TabletFX controls and all-pass/amplitude-modulation processing are project choices, with no Line 6 parity claim.

Vocal FX includes a project-authored split-band De-Esser with Detector Frequency, Q, Threshold, Maximum Reduction, Attack, and Release. HeadRush Prime's current official model list names a De-Esser, but does not expose its controls or ranges in the available source text; these TabletFX parameters and DSP do not claim HeadRush parity. The guitar-fixture browser smoke test verifies the signal path, not voice intelligibility or sound quality.

The browser effect picker filters by category and name. Under Modulation it includes project-authored Auto-Pan (Rate, Depth, square/sine/triangle Type, optional tempo and Tap Division), Harmonic Tremolo (Rate, Depth, crossover, waveform, free/tempo sync, stereo band alternation, and smoothing), Rotary Speaker (with separate low/high mic-spacing controls, rate/tempo, slow/fast acceleration, rotor/drum depth and level, drive, stereo spread, balance, input selection, mix, and output level), a classic project-authored Ring Modulator (frequency, multiplier, high cut, and mix), and a project-authored Rhythmic Slicer with a 16-step level pattern, tempo divisions or free rate, depth, transition shape, attack/release, and phase. Auto-Pan follows the control names listed for Fender Tone Master Pro Orbit Stereo Panner, but the guide gives no ranges/defaults and TabletFX's values and DSP are project-authored; no Fender parity is claimed. Under Filter it includes a project-authored Step Filter with low/band/high-pass and notch modes, a 16-step cutoff pattern, tempo/free rate, cutoff range, resonance, depth, transition shape, smoothing, mix, and phase; a four-band Formant Filter with manual A/E/I/O/U morph, resonance Q, wet mix, and output level; and an eight-mode Harmonic Resonator with root frequency, decay, brightness, and wet mix. These processors do not claim branded DSP emulation.

Under Delay, the categorized picker includes project-authored Auto-Swell, Reverse, Ducking, Modulated, Filtered, Resonant, and Swept Filter Delays. Auto-Swell Delay exposes Time, Feedback, Mix, free/tempo-synced time and Tap Division, Swell, Mod, Tone, Sense, Slow/Normal/Fast Taper, Dry, and Spillover. These are project-authored controls and ranges; Fender's Tone Master Pro Model Guide v1.1.100 names these controls but publishes no numeric ranges or full signal topology, so no Fender DSP/control parity is claimed. Reverse Delay exposes free or tempo-synced Time (20–300 BPM, 12 divisions), Feedback, Mix, Crossfade, repeat Low/High Cut, Forward Level, Run, Trigger Restart, and block-bypass Spillover. These are project controls and ranges; spillover covers block bypass, not preset/scene transitions. Ducking Delay exposes Time, Feedback, Mix, Threshold, Attenuation, and Release, with a stereo-linked input peak detector lowering only the repeats while playing above threshold. Modulated Delay adds Time, Feedback, Mix, LFO Rate and Depth, Sine/Triangle/Random/Triggered Ramp modes, stereo spread, and a trigger threshold. Filtered Delay exposes Time, Feedback, Mix, Low Cut, and High Cut; its project-authored one-pole filters shape repeats in the feedback path while the direct signal stays unchanged. Resonant Delay exposes Time 350 ms, Feedback 0.35, Center 800 Hz, Resonance Q 2, and Mix 0.25; its project-authored band-pass biquad filters delayed repeats before output and feedback. These are not HeadRush Prime RESO DELAY parameters or DSP parity. These controls and ranges do not emulate documented Sweep Delay, Step Filter Delay, Sweep Echo, or RESO DELAY algorithms. The picker also offers a project-authored Swept Filter Delay: Time 350 ms (1–2,000 ms), Feedback 0.25 (0–0.95), Mix 0.25 (0–1), Start/Stop 250/6,000 Hz, Resonance Q 1.2, Rate 0.5 Hz, Sine/Triangle/Square waveform, and 90° stereo phase; project bounds are detailed in the comparison and matrix. A fixed band-pass biquad sweeps the delayed wet output, with an immediate dry path; there is no filter-type selector or tempo sync, and this does not claim vendor parity.

The Delay picker also includes a project-authored Ten-Tap Delay with 1–10 repeats, interval/tempo, decay, shuffle, stereo spread/ratio, pan shape, tone cuts, and per-tap level controls. The documented per-tap bounds are −80 to +20 dB; other controls, bounds, defaults, and buffer capacity are project choices. A separate project-authored Rhythm Tap Delay adds free or quantized relative intervals, tap learning, and final-repeat pattern feedback; its bounds and DSP are project choices, not Fractal parity.

The categorized Delay picker also includes a project-authored 1–64-tap Megatap Delay: last-tap time/count, Time Alpha and randomization, amplitude/pan shapes and randomization, stereo spread, predelay, feedback/feedback tap, tone cuts, input diffusion/LFO, envelope swell, input selection/gain, Mix, and wet Level. The Fractal Blocks Guide documents a four-second 64-tap FM9-family line and these control concepts, but most bounds/defaults and the DSP are not specified; TabletFX's 1 MiB ring, ranges and algorithms are project-authored. FM9 v12.0 is listed for Original/Turbo without separate Mk II Turbo wording, so exact applicability remains qualified. No Fractal DSP, parameter, or sound parity is claimed.
The Delay picker also includes a project-authored four-line Multi-Delay with six topologies: Quad Parallel-like cyclic feedback, Quad Series-like cumulative taps, a four-stage all-pass Diffusor-like chain, Quad-Tap-like independent lines, Quad Tap Band-like output filtering, and Quad Tape-like motor-speed echoes. The tape control uses project-selected 0.8–1.2× bounds and interpolated/slewed read heads; the November 2026 Fractal guide documents 1.000 as normal speed but publishes no Quad Tape numeric range. Its 512 KiB ring limits each line to about 682 ms at 48 kHz. The eight-line Plex Matrix Delay also exposes selected wet Filter/LFO controls, envelope-controlled send, a ducker, and Stereo Spread informed by the FM9-family Plex documentation. Both effects use project-authored DSP and bounds; this is not Fractal DSP, control, or sound parity. Input diffusion, other Multi-Delay/Plex processing pages, and spillover remain partial.


Loop & performance also includes a project-authored Shuffling Looper with sliced sequence playback, shuffle/pitch/reverse/repeat probabilities, smoothing, sequence drift, filters, and two pitch intervals. Its four-second recording limit and control bounds are project choices; its separate browser transport does not copy Helix Stadium's single-switch timing or claim sound/control parity.

Rhythmic Slicer, Step Filter, and both looper controls, test evidence, CPU/memory measurements, and manufacturer comparisons are recorded in [`EFFECTS_MATRIX.md`](EFFECTS_MATRIX.md), [`IMPLEMENTATION_CHECKLIST.md`](IMPLEMENTATION_CHECKLIST.md), and [`FLAGSHIP_EFFECT_COMPARISON.md`](FLAGSHIP_EFFECT_COMPARISON.md). Browser Play runs the same C++ processors through WebAssembly/AudioWorklet.

The processor set also includes optical/FET-style compressors, guitar/bass graphic EQs, and low-shelf/high-shelf/tilt EQ. Automated DSP/CLI tests, per-effect and full-chain CPU/memory benchmarks, and a prerecorded-guitar regression path are available. The browser test page loads a local audio file, exposes a categorized effect picker including Dynamics and Reverb, a ±12-semitone pitch control, expression position/taper/endpoints, per-block bypass, global bypass, input mute, panic mute, input/output peak and RMS meters with clip indicators, Room Reverb size/decay/predelay/damping/mix/level, Hall Reverb decay/predelay/damping/low-cut/mix/level, Plate Reverb decay/wet-predelay/damping/diffusion/early-late balance/mix/level controls, Spring Reverb decay/wet-predelay/damping/dwell/mix/level controls, Auto Swell and Auto-Swell Delay controls, gate/boost/overdrive/high-gain distortion/round-fuzz/expression-wah/touch-wah/tremolo/chorus/flanger/phaser/vibrato/tempo-sync, dual-delay, multi-tap, BBD, Tape Echo, multi-head, optical/FET compressor, guitar/bass graphic EQ, and shelf/tilt EQ controls, and output protection; shared C++ DSP runs through WebAssembly/AudioWorklet, and pressing Play outputs the processed chain for local audio. Run `make web-serve` and open `http://127.0.0.1:8000/web/` to select a local guitar recording, adjust the chain, and press Play; audio stays on this machine. Automated Play capture is compared with the native renderer. Manual audible-output/listening verification remains open. Chrome 151's worklet exposes only a 1 ms timer in this environment, so the browser's live per-effect CPU percentages are coarse. An initial Windows WASAPI backend and MinGW cross-build are present, but the backend is not integrated into the app UI or run on Windows hardware. The native application UI, Android/Linux live audio backends, and hardware configurations remain unverified.
