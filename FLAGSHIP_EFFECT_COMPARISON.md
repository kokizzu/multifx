# Flagship effects comparison and implementation checklist

Research snapshot: **2026-11-22**. This compares manufacturer-documented categories and representative features for the six named flagship processors. It is a focused documentation check, not a fresh audit of every algorithm in every firmware or of the whole effects catalogue. Product names below identify documented examples; TabletFX implementations should use project-authored names, controls, and signal paths rather than copy proprietary presets or imply circuit emulation.

## Evidence rules

- **Documented model/category** means a manufacturer page or guide names a category or effect. It does not by itself verify every parameter, firmware availability, audio quality, or simultaneous use.
- **Parameter evidence** is listed only where a model-specific guide or manual gives controls. Broad controls such as Mix, Level, and Time are not assumed to exist on every algorithm.
- **Unknown** means the available source did not establish the detail; it does not mean the device lacks it.
- Firmware notes identify model additions or changes. A model guide from an older firmware is not a complete current catalogue.
- Captures, amp/cab/IR assets, external loops, routing utilities, tuners, and MIDI/instrument engines are kept distinct from ordinary audio-effect algorithms.

### Melofty Axom Max

**Evidence:** Melofty-branded Axom Max User Manual V1.7.4, dated 2026-08-31; that is the document revision, not proof of installed device firmware. Melofty's official app-store listing has v1.9.2 release notes, which are app notes rather than firmware notes. - **Effect browsing / resources:** The manual describes module categories, repeated slots, two A/B chains with nine slots each, dynamic CPU limits, and serial, parallel, split/merge, A/B-to-Y, and Y-to-A/B routing (pp. 11–12, 17). The linked Warwick product listing says 19 effect modules; a separate manufacturer-linked overview advertises 880+ “high-quality effects,” without defining whether that total includes amps, cabinets, instruments, synth tones, or effect algorithms. Neither source enumerates the full model library or provides per-model control tables.
- **Documented families:** The picker names Pitch, Wah, Mod, Delay, Inst.Sim, and Synth categories. DeepSynth supplies system preset instrument-simulation and synth tones; the manual does not publish voice names or prove guitar-tracked/polyphonic synth input. Delay and modulation support BPM sync. Melofty's official app v1.9.2 notes say Delay timing was expanded to 1/64–1/1 and High/Low Cut were added; they do not give exact UI parameter ranges or establish installed device firmware.
- **Other functions:** The manual also documents a global input Noise Gate, global output EQ, tuner, FX Send/Return/Loop, and a stereo phrase looper with reverse/half-speed and up to 3,600 seconds. MFIM amp/cab captures and cabinet assets are capture/modeling resources, not ordinary effect algorithms or a complete guitar synth inventory.
- **Checklist result:** categories and selected global/looper behavior are documented, but algorithm names, per-effect parameters, synth voices/tracking/polyphony, and exact device firmware remain unverified.

#### Project comparison

The current **50-stage** picker is categorized as Utility, Dynamics, Filters & wah, Drive & fuzz, Modulation, Equalizer, Delay, Pitch and Reverb. It has broad function-family coverage for drive/fuzz, compression/gating/swell, filters/wah, modulation, EQ, delays, pitch shifting and room/hall/plate/spring reverbs. Modulation includes a project-authored Dimension Chorus using Line 6 Stadium’s documented Version C/D and ten Mode labels; its delay/phase profiles and DSP are not a Dimension W/DC-2W emulation. Drive & fuzz includes a project-authored Bit Crusher with signed 2…24-bit quantization and per-channel sample hold; its sample-rate reduction intentionally aliases and has no selectable anti-alias filter. These generic controls do not establish Fractal algorithm, setting, or DSP parity. The Formant Filter is a project-authored four-band manual A/E/I/O/U morph; the Harmonic Resonator is a separate project-authored eight-mode resonant filter bank. These are **category-level coverage only**: no generic stage establishes equivalence to a named Fender model or its controls; neither stage is a synth, pitch-formant processor, or automatic humanizer. The project has no dedicated **Synth**, **Special/Looper**, sequenced pitch, harmonizer, pedal-shifter/detune, feedback-generator, convolution-reverb, or Fender-style stereo doubler model. Its Step Filter, Rhythmic Slicer, Auto Swell, generic pitch shifter and Sweep Delay are related tools, not verified equivalents to Fender Step Filter/Tremolo/Pitch Sequencer, Auto-Swell Delay, Virtual Capo, or the newer delay/reverb models. Do not infer their tracking, latency, texture, parameter ranges, or simultaneous-use limits from Fender's documentation.

### Line 6 Helix Stadium XL

**Evidence:** The currently selected manual/release notes are v1.4.x (hardware firmware v1.4.2, 2026-10-27); the catalogue marks models introduced in 1.4. Stadium XL and Stadium share effect blocks, while XL adds Instrument 1+2 input choices. [Manual selector](https://manuals.line6.com/en/helix-stadium), [current effect-block reference](https://manuals.line6.com/en/helix-stadium/live/effect-blocks), [v1.4 release notes](https://kb.line6.com/helix-stadium-1-4-release-notes), [current model catalogue](https://line6.com/helix-stadium-models/).

- **Categories:** Distortion, Delay, Reverb, Modulation, Dynamics, EQ, Pitch/Synth, Wah/Filter, and Volume/Pan; Looper is separate. Amp/preamp, cab/IR, clones, FX loop, split/merge, and input/output blocks are separately identifiable and should not be counted as stompbox effects.
- **Drive / dynamics / EQ / modulation:** The current catalogue includes boost, overdrive, distortion, and fuzz models, plus compressors, gates, autoswell, Feedbacker, modulation, EQ, and wah/filter categories. Specific model availability must follow each catalogue entry's initial-appearance version.
- **Delay / reverb:** The current model catalogue names Dynamic Hall, Dynamic Plate, Dynamic Room, Dynamic Ambience, Dynamic Bloom, Shimmer, Hot Springs, Nonlinear, Glitz, Ganymede, Searchlights, Plateaux, Double Tank, and legacy Plate/Room/Chamber/Hall/Echo/Tile/Cave/Ducking/Octo/Spring/Particle Verb. Current manual revisions present Reverb as a category spanning room, plate, hall, chamber, spring, shimmer, and nonlinear effects; the catalogue specifically lists Hot Springs, ’63 Spring, and Spring, but the reviewed Stadium material has no per-model spring parameter sheet. the shared effect-parameter table includes Decay, Predelay, Mix, Level, Low Cut, High Cut, and Trails where an individual block supports them. That shared table is not a per-model Dynamic Hall/Plate parameter list, so exact model controls remain block-specific. Helix Stadium's [official catalogue](https://line6.com/helix-stadium-models/) lists Dynamic Hall in mono/stereo; the [current effect-block documentation](https://manuals.line6.com/en/helix-stadium) gives common controls rather than Dynamic Hall internals.
- **Pitch / synth distinction:** The catalogue separates 3 OSC Synth, Poly Pitch/Wham/Capo, Pitch Wham and other tracked pitch effects, 3 Note Generator, and 4 OSC Generator. Do not infer input-note polyphony from oscillator or generated-voice counts. The manual lists common parameter families such as Drive/Gain, Bass/Mid/Treble, Rate/Speed, Depth, Spread, Headroom, Low/High Cut, and Sidechain; they are not controls on every algorithm.
- **New-model parameter evidence:** v1.4 release notes give controls (but no numeric ranges) for six additions: Mercator (Drive, Drive 2, Bass, Mid, Treble, Bright 1/2, Level); Rock Box Dist (Drive, Tone, Level); Rock Box Boost (Boost, Level); PortaBox 424 (Gain 1/2, Bass, Treble, Level); Essex Vibe (Rate, Depth, Trem, Stages, stereo Spread, Level); Dimension W (Version, Mode, Mix, Level). Stadium's public manuals generally rely on in-device parameter descriptions for other algorithms.
- **TabletFX implementation:** `Dimension Chorus` is available under Modulation with Version C/D and all documented single/pair Mode choices. Mix is 0–100%; output Level is −24…+12 dB as a project-authored bound because the release notes do not publish its numerical range. Internal modulation profiles and DSP are project-authored, with no Line 6/BOSS parity claim.
- **Stepped filtering:** The current model catalogue lists Asheville Pattrn, Seeker, and Obi Wah among filter models, but no effect named Step Filter or model-specific control tables for those entries. Similar rhythmic/filter behavior must not be treated as the same algorithm without documentation.
- **DSP caveat:** Rev D v1.3 allows only one effect from the combined Feedbacker / Poly Sustain / Poly Detune / Poly Pitch / Poly Wham / Poly Capo / 12 String group on a given path. This is a per-path limit, not a claim that only one may appear in a whole preset, and DSP still constrains combinations.
- **Checklist result:** strong category and model-name coverage; use the model catalogue's version column for current applicability and the block-specific inspector for exact controls.

Named examples include 77 Custom OD, Face Fuzz, Klone Chiron, Rat Distortion, T808 OD, Octave Distortion, and Bit Crusher. Documented examples include ±50-cent detune, ±24-semitone shifts, 0–250 ms or 0–2000 ms voice delays depending on type, a 16-step Arpeggiator, and formant correction/shift; the Pitch block normally sums its input to mono, while documented Detune and Shift types can use stereo input. Synth is a three-oscillator **monophonic** tracker, not a three-note polyphonic synth. Per-voice parameters include waveform (sine, triangle, square, sawtooth, random, white/pink noise, off), tracking mode, Frequency, Shift, Tune, Duty Cycle, Pan, filter frequency/Q, Attack, and voice Level. Formant selects Start/Middle/End vowels and Resonance, with Control to morph among them. Resonator offers Manual or Chordal mode, four resonant bands, and per-band Frequency, Feedback, Filter Q, Level, and Pan.
- **Delay and reverb:** The 29 ordinary Delay types include Digital/Analog Mono/Stereo, Mono/Stereo Tape, Ping-Pong, Dual, Reverse, Sweep, Ducking, Vintage Digital, 2290 w/ Modulation, Ambient Stereo, Deluxe/Stereo Mind Guy, Mono/Stereo BBD, Lo-Fi Tape, Diffused Delay, Graphite Copy, Worn Tape, Zephyr, DM-Two, Stereo Trem, Wandering, Pan, Dual Head Tape, and Surround Delay. FM9 delay time is documented up to 16 seconds. Reverse Delay documents Time, Run, Trigger Restart, and Crossfade Time. Ducking uses Ducker Atten, Threshold and Release. Sweep Delay is an LFO-controlled stereo band-pass output sweep. The other dedicated blocks are Megatap (22 types; 64 taps on FM9), Multi-Delay (39 types; core algorithms Quad-Tap, Quad Parallel, Quad Series, Quad Diffusor, Quad Tape, Quad Tap Band), Plex Delay (45 types; core Plex Delay, Plex Detune, Plex Shift, Plex Verb, Shimmer Verb, using up to eight delay lines), and Ten-Tap/Rhythm Tap (two types, 1–10 taps). Reverb has 79 types grouped as Chamber, Cloud, Echo, Hall, Huge, Plate, Room, SFX, and Spring; controls include Time, Predelay, Size, frequency-dependent decay, diffusion, early/late levels, and modulation. Pitch shifting is available in some FM9 reverb types; it is explicitly absent from FM3 because of CPU limits.
- **EQ, crossover and stereo:** Graphic EQ has 18 types, with 10/8/7/5-band, passive, console, JMP-1, GE-7 and bass GE-7B variants; band gain/cut is up to ±12 dB. The five-band Parametric EQ supports Shelving, Shelving 2, Peaking, Peaking 2 and Blocking band types; documented ranges include Q 0.1–10, gain −20 to +20 dB, and slope 6–36 dB/octave for Blocking filters. Crossover is a two-way stereo fourth-order Linkwitz-Riley split, with frequency 40–800 Hz or 400 Hz–8 kHz using ×10; two blocks can form a three-way split. Enhancer offers Modern, Classic and Stereoizer; documented controls include Width/Depth, Low/High Cut, Phase Invert, left/right pan, and (Classic) 0–20 ms right-channel delay.

#### Resource, latency, and parity limits

The guide's FM9 instance counts are per preset, not a guarantee that every block can run at once. The FM9 uses a flexible grid, but available DSP and block-specific costs constrain a preset. The older v3.x Owner's Manual recommends about 80% CPU load; above the allowable limit, audio processing is muted. It notes that parameter choices also matter: Phaser stages, Filter Order/Q, Multi-Delay type, Plex delay count, and active Synth voices affect CPU; reverb quality settings trade CPU for smoother tails. Treat the 80% guidance as the 2022 manual's recommendation, not a verified v12-specific threshold. The current guide documents no general end-to-end latency figure; Compressor Look-Ahead Time is 0–2 ms and explicitly adds actual latency to both wet and dry output.

**TabletFX match status:** The picker has generic Dynamics; Filters & Wah; Drive & Fuzz; Modulation; Equalizer; Delay; Pitch; Reverb; and Utility stages. Shared broad functions—such as Chorus, Flanger, Phaser, Tremolo, Rotary, Ring Modulator, Wah, EQ, pitch shift, Reverse/Ducking/Sweep Delay, and the four basic reverb families—are **related, not equivalent** to Fractal's named types and controls. A project-authored four-band manual A/E/I/O/U Formant Filter is present; it is not Fractal's Formant voice control, does not shift formants on pitched voices, and has no automatic humanizer. The project-authored eight-mode Harmonic Resonator is a bank of integer-harmonic band-pass filters with Root Frequency, Decay, Brightness, and Mix. Fractal's documented Resonator instead offers Manual/Chordal modes and four bands with per-band Frequency, Feedback, Filter Q, Level, and Pan. Thus the generic filter bank is a related sound-design tool, not that algorithm; the Fractal-style Resonator remains **missing**. Dedicated Enhancer, Crossover, Synth, Multiband Compressor, Megatap, Multi-Delay, Plex Delay, and Ten-Tap algorithms also remain **missing** from the picker. TabletFX Sweep Delay is project-authored: its current implementation is a wet-output band-pass biquad sweep with an immediate dry path; it has no Fractal Filter Type selector or Sweep Tempo, so this shared name does not establish vendor parity. Whether TabletFX's combined Digital Delay stage internally covers subsets of Fractal's other delay blocks is **unknown from the picker**.

## Recommended implementation order

This order favors categories documented across the named units, while limiting DSP cost and keeping distinct algorithms separate. It is a work queue, not a claim that all processors share identical models or controls.

1. **Room reverb (`REV-01`) — implemented foundation.** Native C++, offline CLI, browser/WASM picker, prerecorded audio regression, and Windows cross-build are covered; automated tests and CPU/memory measurement pass. The algorithm is project-authored and does not claim a commercial room model. Active-tail response when changing room size and human listening remain open.
2. **Hall (`REV-02`) — implemented and validated.** The project-authored algorithm has separate native and browser stages, controls, tests, recorded-guitar regression, Windows cross-build, and CPU/memory figures. It is not represented as a Helix Stadium Dynamic Hall or Fender convolution-Hall clone; exact commercial model internals remain unpublished.
3. **Plate (`REV-03`) — implemented and validated.** The project-authored plate-style FDN is not a Fender Plate model or Helix Dynamic Plate clone. **Spring (`REV-04…05`)** is next; keep convolution assets and hardware amp spring models separate.
4. **Auto Swell (`DYN-13`) — implemented and validated.** Fractal's guide documents threshold, attack, release, hysteresis, taper, and volume controls but omits numeric limits and taper curves. This project uses its own detector and curve approximations.
5. **Rotary Speaker (`MOD-15`) — implemented and validated.** The Fractal guide establishes the separate low/high mic-spacing and motor-time-constant controls, but does not publish numeric limits or mappings. TabletFX uses its own crossover, bounded delay, motor model, and normalized controls; listening and hardware comparisons remain open.
6. **Ring modulation (`MOD-17`) — classic mode implemented; pitch tracking and SSB remain open.** The Fractal guide names Classic, two SSB types, Frequency Multiplier, and Pitch Tracking; the Helix Stadium catalogue separately lists AM Ring Mod, Pitch Ring Mod, and a legacy Ring Modulator. No shared proprietary controls or sound are assumed.
7. **Rhythmic slicer (`MOD-16`), sequenced filter (`EQF-14`), and static notch (`EQF-06`) — implemented and automated.** Their patterns, controls, timing, browser parity, prerecorded input, CPU, and memory are recorded in `EFFECTS_MATRIX.md` and `IMPLEMENTATION_CHECKLIST.md`; manual listening remains open. The reverse-delay manufacturer comparison is recorded above; the partial prototype and its remaining controls/validation are tracked in `DLY-09`. Static filtered delay is DLY-12; the additional swept-filter delay is DLY-20. A tracked monophonic synth remains a separate research lead.
8. **Delay variants (`DLY-09…20`)** — Reverse Delay (`DLY-09`) now has tempo division, repeat cuts, forward tap, and block-bypass spillover with automated native/browser/CLI, recorded-audio, benchmark, and Windows cross-build evidence; live device listening remains open. Ducking Delay (`DLY-10`), Modulated Delay (`DLY-11`), static Filtered Delay (`DLY-12`), and swept-filter delay (`DLY-20`, approved addition) have automated evidence. DLY-13 remains the separate pitch-shifted-feedback delay, followed by swell, resonant, granular, hold/freeze, lo-fi, and bounded self-oscillation. Keep each manufacturer comparison scoped to its exact algorithm, controls, and evidence level; preserve safe feedback/memory and document latency/tail behavior.
9. **Pitch/synth and vocal families (`PIT-*`, `SYN-*`, `DYN-16`)** — higher tracking, latency, polyphony, and resource risk. Require chord tests, latency reporting, and a strict distinction among mono tracking, polyphonic audio transposition, sampled tones, oscillators, and MIDI triggering.

For each implementation, update the relevant row in `IMPLEMENTATION_CHECKLIST.md` only after native tests and browser/Windows integration checks pass. Record Linux CPU as a percentage of the callback deadline, fixed state/memory, algorithmic latency, and manual listening status in the project's existing benchmark/evidence documents.
