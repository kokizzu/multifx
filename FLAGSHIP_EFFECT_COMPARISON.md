# Flagship effects comparison and implementation checklist

Research snapshot: **2026-11-21**. This compares manufacturer-documented categories and representative features for the six named flagship processors. It is a focused documentation check, not a fresh audit of every algorithm in every firmware or of the whole effects catalogue. Product names below identify documented examples; TabletFX implementations should use project-authored names, controls, and signal paths rather than copy proprietary presets or imply circuit emulation.

## Evidence rules

- **Documented model/category** means a manufacturer page or guide names a category or effect. It does not by itself verify every parameter, firmware availability, audio quality, or simultaneous use.
- **Parameter evidence** is listed only where a model-specific guide or manual gives controls. Broad controls such as Mix, Level, and Time are not assumed to exist on every algorithm.
- **Unknown** means the available source did not establish the detail; it does not mean the device lacks it.
- Firmware notes identify model additions or changes. A model guide from an older firmware is not a complete current catalogue.
- Captures, amp/cab/IR assets, external loops, routing utilities, tuners, and MIDI/instrument engines are kept distinct from ordinary audio-effect algorithms.

## Source-backed comparison by model

### Melofty Axom Max

**Evidence:** Melofty-branded Axom Max User Manual V1.7.4, dated 2026-08-31; that is the document revision, not proof of installed device firmware. Melofty's official app-store listing has v1.9.2 release notes, which are app notes rather than firmware notes. [User Manual V1.7.4](https://cdn.rockboard.de/nos/data/erp/Artikell_PDF/Manual_PDF/Melofty/EN/MEL_AXOM_MAX_Manual_EN.pdf), [MELOFTY app release history](https://apps.apple.com/sr/app/melofty/id6762972347)

- **Effect browsing / resources:** The manual describes module categories, repeated slots, two A/B chains with nine slots each, dynamic CPU limits, and serial, parallel, split/merge, A/B-to-Y, and Y-to-A/B routing (pp. 11–12, 17). It claims 19 modules and 880+ effect models but does not enumerate the full model library or provide per-model control tables.
- **Documented families:** The picker names Pitch, Wah, Mod, Delay, Inst.Sim, and Synth categories. DeepSynth supplies system preset instrument-simulation and synth tones; the manual does not publish voice names or prove guitar-tracked/polyphonic synth input. Delay and modulation support BPM sync. Melofty's official app v1.9.2 notes say Delay timing was expanded to 1/64–1/1 and High/Low Cut were added; they do not give exact UI parameter ranges or establish installed device firmware.
- **Other functions:** The manual also documents a global input Noise Gate, global output EQ, tuner, FX Send/Return/Loop, and a stereo phrase looper with reverse/half-speed and up to 3,600 seconds. MFIM amp/cab captures and cabinet assets are capture/modeling resources, not ordinary effect algorithms or a complete guitar synth inventory.
- **Checklist result:** categories and selected global/looper behavior are documented, but algorithm names, per-effect parameters, synth voices/tracking/polyphony, and exact device firmware remain unverified.

### Fender Tone Master Pro

**Evidence:** Fender's linked Model Guide is for firmware **v1.1.100** and predates later step effects. The rolling firmware notes identify **v1.8.85 (2026-11-18)** as the latest checked release; it updates names/Virtual Capo but does not add or remove the step effects listed in earlier notes. [Product page and model guide](https://www.fender.com/products/tone-master-pro), [Model Guide PDF](https://www.fmicassets.com/Damroot/Original/10018/OM_2274900000_TONE-MASTER-PRO-Model-Guide_English.pdf), [firmware and Pro Control release notes](https://support.fender.com/hc/en-us/articles/46768129660315-Fender-Tone-Master-Pro-Firmware-and-Pro-Control-App-Updates).

- **Drive / fuzz:** The guide categorizes Stompbox effects and documents examples such as Greenbox (Level, Overdrive, Tone, Blend), Pugilist Distortion (two gain/tone engines, Level, Blend), and Maximus Drive (Gain, Resonance, EQ, Boost).
- **Dynamics / EQ / filters:** The v1.1.100 Model Guide used combined Dynamics + EQ and Filters + Pitch headings. Firmware v1.7.53 later split Dynamics, EQ, Filter, and Pitch into separate Add/Replace categories and added Synth. The current taxonomy is Stompbox, Modulation, Delay, Reverb, Dynamics, EQ, Filter, Pitch, Synth. Documented older-model examples include pedal/studio/simple/dynamic compressors, Sustain, parametric/graphic EQ, gates, Volume Pedal, Auto Swell, custom/Teardrop/Vocal Wah, and feedback generation. Studio Compressor exposes Gain, Threshold, Ratio, Attack, Release, Knee, and Blend; do not transfer its controls to newer models without model-specific evidence.
- **Modulation:** Examples include Tremolo (Rate, Intensity, Duty Cycle, tap division, Shape, LFO Clip), Vibratone (Drive, Tone, Level, fast/slow speeds, ramp, sensitivity), Flanger Mono (Rate, Depth, Resonance, Manual), and Uni-Verse Vibe (Speed, Intensity, Volume, tap division, modes).
- **Delay / reverb:** Delay models include digital, dynamic, echo-filter, reverse, and stereo types with model-specific timing/feedback/mix and modulation/filter controls. The older v1.1.100 guide covers then-current spring, hall, room, plate, Cloud, Celestial, Nebula, and Shimmer models; for example, Large Plate exposes Mix, Decay, Diffuse, High/Low Damp, High/Low Tone, Balance, and Spillover. Official release notes list Small Room Reverb, Small Plate Reverb, Ascension and Imperium Convolution Hall, Kinetic Convolution Plate, and Atmosphere Convolution Room in v1.6.34; v1.7.75 adds Vast Convolution Chamber, Ethereal and Retrograde Convolution Hall, and Alkaline Convolution Plate. The latest firmware checked is v1.8.85. Those release notes do not publish these added models' parameter tables, so do not copy controls from the older guide. The one-convolution-reverb-per-preset limit is documented for the additions, including amps with built-in convolution reverb. The older v1.1.100 guide’s Spring Reverb entry documents Mixer, Dwell, Tone, and Spillover; these are guide/model-specific controls, not a parameter sheet for later convolution reverbs.
- **Pitch / synth / firmware changes:** The guide describes Chromatic Pitch Shifter and Diatonic Harmonizer as typically best with single notes; that is not evidence of polyphonic tracking. Firmware v1.8.85 renames Lightyear Overdrive to Greer Amps Lightspeed and describes Virtual Capo as a polyphonic low-latency ±12-semitone shifter; Resolution and Tracking controls were removed, while Mix is documented without a public numeric range. v1.8.58 adds Aethon, Cerberus, and Wavemorph Polysynths without publishing their parameter tables. v1.8.45 adds Step Tremolo, Step Filter, Step Filter Delay, and Pitch Sequencer; v1.8.58 later fixes potential crashes in the step effects. Fender's Step Filter promotion describes rhythmic sweeps, pulses, and morphs but publishes no per-model control list or ranges. The v1.1.100 Model Guide predates these effects.
- **Checklist result:** many families and representative parameters are explicitly documented, but the v1.1.100 guide and release notes must be combined carefully. Do not infer controls for newer synths or step effects from older models.

### Line 6 Helix Stadium XL

**Evidence:** The currently selected manual/release notes are v1.4.x (hardware firmware v1.4.2, 2026-10-27); the catalogue marks models introduced in 1.4. Stadium XL and Stadium share effect blocks, while XL adds Instrument 1+2 input choices. [Manual selector](https://manuals.line6.com/en/helix-stadium), [current effect-block reference](https://manuals.line6.com/en/helix-stadium/live/effect-blocks), [v1.4 release notes](https://kb.line6.com/helix-stadium-1-4-release-notes), [current model catalogue](https://line6.com/helix-stadium-models/).

- **Categories:** Distortion, Delay, Reverb, Modulation, Dynamics, EQ, Pitch/Synth, Wah/Filter, and Volume/Pan; Looper is separate. Amp/preamp, cab/IR, clones, FX loop, split/merge, and input/output blocks are separately identifiable and should not be counted as stompbox effects.
- **Drive / dynamics / EQ / modulation:** The current catalogue includes boost, overdrive, distortion, and fuzz models, plus compressors, gates, autoswell, Feedbacker, modulation, EQ, and wah/filter categories. Specific model availability must follow each catalogue entry's initial-appearance version.
- **Delay / reverb:** The current model catalogue names Dynamic Hall, Dynamic Plate, Dynamic Room, Dynamic Ambience, Dynamic Bloom, Shimmer, Hot Springs, Nonlinear, Glitz, Ganymede, Searchlights, Plateaux, Double Tank, and legacy Plate/Room/Chamber/Hall/Echo/Tile/Cave/Ducking/Octo/Spring/Particle Verb. Current manual revisions present Reverb as a category spanning room, plate, hall, chamber, spring, shimmer, and nonlinear effects; the catalogue specifically lists Hot Springs, ’63 Spring, and Spring, but the reviewed Stadium material has no per-model spring parameter sheet. the shared effect-parameter table includes Decay, Predelay, Mix, Level, Low Cut, High Cut, and Trails where an individual block supports them. That shared table is not a per-model Dynamic Hall/Plate parameter list, so exact model controls remain block-specific. Helix Stadium's [official catalogue](https://line6.com/helix-stadium-models/) lists Dynamic Hall in mono/stereo; the [current effect-block documentation](https://manuals.line6.com/en/helix-stadium) gives common controls rather than Dynamic Hall internals.
- **Pitch / synth distinction:** The catalogue separates 3 OSC Synth, Poly Pitch/Wham/Capo, Pitch Wham and other tracked pitch effects, 3 Note Generator, and 4 OSC Generator. Do not infer input-note polyphony from oscillator or generated-voice counts. The manual lists common parameter families such as Drive/Gain, Bass/Mid/Treble, Rate/Speed, Depth, Spread, Headroom, Low/High Cut, and Sidechain; they are not controls on every algorithm.
- **New-model parameter evidence:** v1.4 release notes give controls (but no numeric ranges) for six additions: Mercator (Drive, Drive 2, Bass, Mid, Treble, Bright 1/2, Level); Rock Box Dist (Drive, Tone, Level); Rock Box Boost (Boost, Level); PortaBox 424 (Gain 1/2, Bass, Treble, Level); Essex Vibe (Rate, Depth, Trem, Stages, stereo Spread, Level); Dimension W (Version, Mode, Mix, Level). Stadium's public manuals generally rely on in-device parameter descriptions for other algorithms.
- **Stepped filtering:** The current model catalogue lists Asheville Pattrn, Seeker, and Obi Wah among filter models, but no effect named Step Filter or model-specific control tables for those entries. Similar rhythmic/filter behavior must not be treated as the same algorithm without documentation.
- **DSP caveat:** Rev D v1.3 allows only one effect from the combined Feedbacker / Poly Sustain / Poly Detune / Poly Pitch / Poly Wham / Poly Capo / 12 String group on a given path. This is a per-path limit, not a claim that only one may appear in a whole preset, and DSP still constrains combinations.
- **Checklist result:** strong category and model-name coverage; use the model catalogue's version column for current applicability and the block-specific inspector for exact controls.

### Fractal FM9 Mark II Turbo

**Evidence:** Fractal's FM9 downloads page lists firmware v12.0 (2026-07-07) as compatible with FM9 Original and FM9 Turbo; it does not identify Mark II Turbo separately. The currently linked Blocks Guide cover is dated November 2026 and covers several Fractal platforms; it is a broad block reference, not v12 release documentation. Use it for documented block types and controls, not as proof that every firmware/model setting or range is unchanged. The FM9 Owner's Manual is v3.x (2022). [FM9 Mark II Turbo product page](https://shop.fractalaudio.com/products/fm9-mark-ii-turbo-amp-modeler-multi-fx-floor-unit?sort=newest), [FM9 downloads / firmware compatibility](https://www.fractalaudio.com/fm9-downloads/), [Fractal Audio Blocks Guide](https://www.fractalaudio.com/downloads/manuals/fas-guides/Fractal-Audio-Blocks-Guide.pdf), [FM9 Owner's Manual](https://www.fractalaudio.com/downloads/manuals/FM9/FM9-Owners-Manual.pdf), [Fractal Mark II Turbo announcement](https://www.fractalaudio.com/about/).

- **Documented categories:** Chorus, Compressor, Crossover, Delay, Drive, Enhancer, Filter, Flanger, Formant, Gate/Expander, Graphic EQ, Megatap, Mixer, Multitap, Multiband Compressor, Multiplexer, Parametric EQ, Phaser, Pitch Shift, Plex Delay, Resonator, Reverb, Ring Mod, Rotary, Synth, Ten-Tap Delay, Tremolo/Panner, Volume/Pan, and Wah. The linked guide's FM9 table describes four Filter instances (15 listed types), one Pitch block, one Synth block, and FM9-specific instance limits; its catalog is not represented here as a current v12 model count.
- **Pitch / harmony:** The older guide describes Chromatic, Diatonic, Detune, Whammy, Virtual Capo, Arpeggiator, and other pitch algorithms. It distinguishes monophonic Diatonic tracking from Chromatic modes and gives a single Pitch-block resource boundary. Later firmware notes have changed tracking descriptions, so old guide ranges/behavior are not asserted as current v12 settings. Polyphonic pitch shifting remains distinct from a polyphonic tracking synthesizer.
- **Synth / instrument simulation:** The guide documents one FM9 Synth block with three oscillator voices and monophonic input tracking; oscillator count does not establish input-note polyphony. Controls include oscillator waveform/noise type, Guitar/Bass tracking selection, Track modes, Frequency, Shift, Tune, Duty Cycle, Pan, filter Frequency/Q, Attack, and voice Level. This is not chord-polyphonic. No dedicated acoustic-instrument simulator was found in the checked guide; that is a bounded documentation finding, not a definitive product-level No.
- **Filters / special:** The guide lists 15 Filter types including Notch and provides generic Type, Frequency, Order, and Q controls; it gives no Notch-specific numeric ranges. Gain applies to shelf/peaking types. The Filter LFO has Enable, Type, Rate, Duty, Tempo, Mod Frequency, and Quantize controls, but the guide does not show a user-edited per-step table. Formant vowel movement and Resonator chordal/metallic comb textures are distinct from note-tracking harmony. Ring Mod documents Classic/SSB Upper/SSB Lower, Frequency, Frequency Multiplier, optional Pitch Tracking, and Hi Cut; the guide's ranges are older and are not asserted as current v12 values.
- **Checklist result:** exact FM9 v12 additions and model-specific current ranges remain unverified from the linked public manual. The older guide documents a stationary Notch type, but not current numeric limits. FM9 Turbo compatibility is documented; Mark II Turbo is not named in that compatibility text.

### HeadRush Prime

**Evidence:** The official Downloads page lists Prime firmware/User Guide v5.2.0; the update notes are dated October 2026 and describe NAM performance/bug fixes, with no effect additions/removals. The 11.7 MB manual exceeds the available document-fetch limit, so its per-effect parameter tables and ranges could not be inspected. [Prime product page](https://www.headrushfx.com/products/prime/), [official downloads](https://www.headrushfx.com/downloads), [Prime User Guide v5.2 PDF](https://cdn.inmusicbrands.com/Software/SGEE/52/HeadRush%20Prime%20-%20User%20Guide%20-%20v5.2.0.pdf), [v5.2 update notes](https://cdn.inmusicbrands.com/Software/SGEE/52/READ%20ME%20-%20HeadRush%20Prime%20Firmware%20Update%20Instructions%20v5.2.0.pdf).

- **Categories and names on the Prime page:** Vocal FX, Overdrive, Distortion/Fuzz, EQ, Compressor, Delay, Reverb, Chorus, Phaser/Flanger, Vib/Trem/Rotary, Wah/Filter, Synth, Rhythmic, Pitch, Volume/Dynamics, and Utility. Pitch entries include Up Tune, Chord Wham, Drop Tune, Harm, Octaves, Octaves Up, Smart Harm, and Wham; synth entries include Ambi-Verb, Hybrid Synth, Electric Piano, and DB-33 Organ; rhythmic entries are Kill Seq and Splicer; Volume/Dynamics includes Autoswell, Feedback, Hold, Volume, and Sustain. The product-page counts conflict with its own list in Overdrive and Pitch, and it omits the 5.1 Graphic Bass EQ addition, so those displayed counts are not an audited inventory. Effect names do not establish input-note polyphony or guitar-tracked synthesis.
- **Firmware additions:** v5.0.0 adds Kill Seq/Splicer, Spring Guru, and arpeggiator controls to Hybrid Synth, DB-33 Organ, and Electric Piano. v5.0.1 adds Drop Tune's Legacy and Refined modes (the notes describe Refined as faster/better tracking); v5.1 adds Graphic Bass EQ and BBD Delay's Classic/Mod mode. v5.2 does not list new or removed effects. The product page has count/list mismatches (for example Overdrive says 16 but lists 18, and Pitch says 7 but lists 8 with a duplicate Octaves label); do not treat those counts as an audited inventory.
- **Rig resources:** Prime supports up to 14 blocks in a rig; official signal paths include 2 vocal + 12 guitar blocks, 4 vocal + 10 guitar blocks, and a dual-guitar layout with 7 FX blocks per input. Delay and reverb tails can spill across rig changes. See the [Prime FAQ](https://support.headrushfx.com/en/support/solutions/articles/69000834532-headrush-prime-frequently-asked-questions) and [signal-path reference](https://support.headrushfx.com/en/support/solutions/articles/69000834956-headrush-prime-what-are-the-new-signal-paths).
- **Spring:** The Prime product page calls its Spring Reverb Fender-inspired; it does not publish the exact controls. Do not transfer Spring controls from the separate list for Pedalboard, Gigboard, and MX5.
- **Parameter evidence:** Prime-specific Vocal Harmony support articles document Lead Level, Harmony Level, Humanize, Auto-Src, Scale Key/Type, Octave Split, delay sync, Stereo, EQ, plus per-voice on/off, Mode, Pitch, Level, Pan, Gender, Delay, and Smooth. Guitar can drive the harmony source, but this is vocal harmony following guitar chords, not proof that guitar pitch effects track chords. The readable official v5.0 guide shows Orange Phaser Rate/Stereo/Sync and Eleven Reverb Decay/Tone/Pre-Delay/Mix/Tails as editor examples; these are not numeric-range tables. C-Verb controls are High Pass, Low Pass, Wet, and Tails, with one C-Verb per rig and IR-length/DSP constraints. The full v5.2 manual is listed officially but was not parseable here; do not borrow Pedalboard/Gigboard/MX5 effect tables.
- **Checklist result:** current Prime effect families and firmware additions are documented, and some vocal/editor controls are verified. Numeric parameter ranges and the complete per-model v5.2 parameter inventory remain unverified. [Vocal Harmony setup](https://support.headrushfx.com/en/support/solutions/articles/69000855449-headrush-setup-and-troubleshooting-the-vocal-harmony-effect), [guitar as Vocal Harmony source](https://support.headrushfx.com/en/support/solutions/articles/69000839530-headrush-prime-controlling-vocal-harmonies-with-your-guitar)

### XRecer XR1

**Evidence:** The official product page, Nov 19 launch article, and FAQ were checked on 2026-11-21. The manual link leads to a wiki that returned no readable manual content, and the download center exposes only a Windows ASIO driver, not a readable full FX manual. The FAQ describes currently installed OS 1.0.0; the launch article describes planned firmware 1.1 vocal/model additions separately. [XR1 product page](https://www.xrecer.com/products/xr1), [Nov 19 launch article](https://www.xrecer.com/blogs/article/xrecer-unveils-xr1-a-new-era-of-multi-effects), [official FAQ](https://www.xrecer.com/pages/faq), [download center](https://www.xrecer.com/pages/download), [manual wiki](https://wiki.xrecer.com/).

- **Listed categories:** overdrive, EQ, dynamics, modulation, reverb, pitch, filters, delay, wah, volume, vocal, loop, IR Loader, FX Loop, CapX, NAM, and NAM Lite. The launch article reports firmware 1.0 counts of 50 amps, 31 drives, 13 cabinets, 21 modulation effects, 16 delays, 14 reverbs, and 11 dynamics processors, with Wah/Pitch/EQ/Filter also included in its stated 187 built-in amps/cabs/effects. The FAQ separately says its manual Effects List has 50 amp entries, 13 base cabinet models, and 111 effect model entries (plus IR Loader/FX Loop entries), so the totals and inventory boundaries conflict.
- **Resource/routing notes:** the FAQ describes up to four chains with eight blocks each, subject to CPU availability, and an on-device CPU indicator. Each chain has its own input noise gate; the device also has a five-band Global EQ, separate from preset effects. These are routing/resource features, not proof that every effect can run simultaneously.
- **Effect vs assets/routing:** IR Loader, CapX, NAM/NAM Lite, and FX Loop are separate asset/capture/routing families in the displayed taxonomy; do not count each as a built-in effect algorithm without detailed manual evidence. `Loop` remains ambiguous between an audio effect and a utility from the available category description.
- **Firmware boundary and checklist result:** launch article says firmware 1.1 is planned for the shipping date with full vocal processing and about 50 more amps/effects, and explicitly says a guitar synth is in development. Those are planned/future claims, not verified installed OS 1.0 effects. Exact algorithm names, control names/ranges, and installed firmware remain unknown; FAQ/launch totals conflict. Do not classify IR Loader, CapX, NAM/NAM Lite, or FX Loop as ordinary effect algorithms.

## Recommended implementation order

This order favors categories documented across the named units, while limiting DSP cost and keeping distinct algorithms separate. It is a work queue, not a claim that all processors share identical models or controls.

1. **Room reverb (`REV-01`) — implemented foundation.** Native C++, offline CLI, browser/WASM picker, prerecorded audio regression, and Windows cross-build are covered; automated tests and CPU/memory measurement pass. The algorithm is project-authored and does not claim a commercial room model. Active-tail response when changing room size and human listening remain open.
2. **Hall (`REV-02`) — implemented and validated.** The project-authored algorithm has separate native and browser stages, controls, tests, recorded-guitar regression, Windows cross-build, and CPU/memory figures. It is not represented as a Helix Stadium Dynamic Hall or Fender convolution-Hall clone; exact commercial model internals remain unpublished.
3. **Plate (`REV-03`) — implemented and validated.** The project-authored plate-style FDN is not a Fender Plate model or Helix Dynamic Plate clone. **Spring (`REV-04…05`)** is next; keep convolution assets and hardware amp spring models separate.
4. **Auto Swell (`DYN-13`) — implemented and validated.** Fractal's guide documents threshold, attack, release, hysteresis, taper, and volume controls but omits numeric limits and taper curves. This project uses its own detector and curve approximations.
5. **Rotary Speaker (`MOD-15`) — implemented and validated.** The Fractal guide establishes the separate low/high mic-spacing and motor-time-constant controls, but does not publish numeric limits or mappings. TabletFX uses its own crossover, bounded delay, motor model, and normalized controls; listening and hardware comparisons remain open.
6. **Ring modulation (`MOD-17`) — classic mode implemented; pitch tracking and SSB remain open.** The Fractal guide names Classic, two SSB types, Frequency Multiplier, and Pitch Tracking; the Helix Stadium catalogue separately lists AM Ring Mod, Pitch Ring Mod, and a legacy Ring Modulator. No shared proprietary controls or sound are assumed.
7. **Rhythmic slicer (`MOD-16`), sequenced filter (`EQF-14`), and static notch (`EQF-06`) — implemented and automated.** Their patterns, controls, timing, browser parity, prerecorded input, CPU, and memory are recorded in `EFFECTS_MATRIX.md` and `IMPLEMENTATION_CHECKLIST.md`; manual listening remains open. The reverse-delay manufacturer comparison is recorded above; the partial prototype and its remaining controls/validation are tracked in `DLY-09`, as are filtered delay and a tracked monophonic synth.
8. **Delay variants (`DLY-09…19`)** — Reverse Delay remains a validated partial prototype; Fender-specific tap subdivision, wet high/low cuts, forward-repeat level, and spillover remain open. Ducking Delay (`DLY-10`) and project-authored Modulated Delay (`DLY-11`) now pass native, offline CLI, browser/WASM, recorded-audio, benchmark, and Windows cross-build checks. Next: filtered delay (`DLY-12`), then pitch-shifted feedback, swell, resonant, granular, hold/freeze, lo-fi, and bounded self-oscillation. Keep each manufacturer comparison scoped to its exact algorithm, controls, and evidence level; preserve safe feedback/memory and document latency/tail behavior.
9. **Pitch/synth and vocal families (`PIT-*`, `SYN-*`, `DYN-16`)** — higher tracking, latency, polyphony, and resource risk. Require chord tests, latency reporting, and a strict distinction among mono tracking, polyphonic audio transposition, sampled tones, oscillators, and MIDI triggering.

For each implementation, update the relevant row in `IMPLEMENTATION_CHECKLIST.md` only after native tests and browser/Windows integration checks pass. Record Linux CPU as a percentage of the callback deadline, fixed state/memory, algorithmic latency, and manual listening status in the project's existing benchmark/evidence documents.
